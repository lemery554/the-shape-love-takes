# Bennett House Continuity Map

**Status:** Working canon for spatial continuity. Readers do not see this map. Exact dimensions may change, but scene routes must not.

## Exterior and site

The Bennetts live in an ordinary two-story, middle-class suburban house with an attached two-car garage on the right. The backyard meets a narrow band of trees. The front door has a keypad. Brooke's upstairs rear bedroom sits above the kitchen-side porch roof, giving her a dangerous but plausible escape route.

## Critical routes

### Anna's school-day search

Anna correlates Brooke's public identity and school exposure with the neighborhood in an old snow-day bus-route video and a cached county-property preview showing the Bennett frontage and part of the street number. She observes the house until Sarah and the girls leave, then uses the keypad code saved from an old public family video. She enters through the front door on two school days, closing it behind her each time. On the first visit, Monday Day 20, her efficient route is living-room photographs, then upstairs to Brooke's room, where she studies the layout and locates the adoption materials in the keepsake box at the foot of Brooke's bed without taking the journal, leaving the adoption-papers container slightly ajar. Brooke later moves the journal into that same box. On the second visit, Tuesday Day 21, Anna returns to the known container, reads the original letter and private entries, and takes the journal. She leaves by the front door both times.

### Sarah and the gun

From Brooke's closed door, Sarah goes down the stairs, crosses the foyer, enters the garage through the mudroom, opens Mark's secured electronic safe, and returns by the same route. Mark previously required Sarah to learn the combination for household emergencies, so her access is retained family knowledge rather than unexplained skill. The safe contains his twelve-gauge pump shotgun, stored with 2¾-inch low-recoil No. 4 buckshot. The distance creates the siege's long silence.

### Bedroom siege and escape

Brooke pushes her desk chair and a low dresser against the inward-opening bedroom door. Before firing, Sarah hears the open window and Cooper's claws on the sill, orders Claire downstairs, and knows Brooke has moved away from her prior position, but cannot know her exact body position through the wall. Sarah tells Brooke to remain below the window because she "only" needs the latch, making the unsafe rationalization explicit. She fires once from the hall, low and diagonally at the latch-side jamb, away from the room's center. The shot destroys the latch, damages the dresser corner, and strikes the floor and side wall. Her intent is to breach the barrier and regain access, but firing through an opaque barrier still creates lethal risk and proves that possessive protection can justify violence. Brooke lowers herself from the rear window onto the shallow porch roof, crosses to its low edge, drops into the backyard, and runs toward the side gate or woods. The second 911 call is open for the shot and upgrades to priority; a nearby car alarm, porch lights, and a neighbor raising a phone acknowledge the blast. No police arrive before Brooke escapes because the first call was queued as a welfare return during reduced staffing and the armed upgrade occurs only moments before Anna's pickup.

## Sound map

- The stairwell carries voices between the kitchen, foyer, and bedroom hall.
- The garage safe is normally inaudible upstairs, but its metal door and dropped tool carry in the unnatural silence.
- Bathroom lock damage carries to the landing but is muffled downstairs.
- Scratching at Brooke's door is intimate and localized; pounding travels through the framing.
- A car in the driveway can be heard from Claire's front room before Brooke's rear room.

## Light map

- Morning light reaches the kitchen and Brooke's rear window.
- Streetlights stripe Claire's front room and the upstairs landing.
- The hall has a weak night-light near the bathroom, useful for a reflective green-eye effect.
- The garage uses harsh overhead LEDs over Mark's bench and safe.
- The backyard and porch roof become nearly black beyond the kitchen spill light.

## Continuity rules

- Brooke cannot see the front door from her bed, but she can hear it through the stairwell.
- Sarah needs several minutes to retrieve the gun; the silence must feel long enough to offer false relief.
- Brooke's escape window is established during ordinary family life before the siege.
- The keypad is visible from the foyer-facing family-video angle without the video deliberately featuring it.
- The house begins as a warm, mildly cluttered home. Its routes become threatening only after familiar people redefine safety.
