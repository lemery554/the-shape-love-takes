# Phase 7 Manuscript Style Sheet

**Parent:** GitHub issue #30

**Provenance:** Created through an AI-assisted internal copyedit. This is not a professional human copyedit and must not be represented as one.

## Editorial baseline

- American English, with Chicago-style fiction conventions and Merriam-Webster spellings.
- Use the serial comma.
- Preserve fragments used for voice, tension, lists, and observed detail.
- Preserve the manuscript's no-em-dash style.
- Source Markdown uses straight quotation marks and apostrophes. Typographic conversion, if desired, belongs to production.
- Use three periods for interrupted or degraded recordings: `...`
- Preferred forms include `gray`, `okay`, `toward`, and `canceled`.

## Numbers, ages, dates, and times

- Spell general narrative numbers through one hundred and rounded larger numbers.
- Use numerals for exact clock readings, temperatures, years, interface data, logs, and `911`.
- Exact times use `6:42`, with `a.m.` or `p.m.` when context does not establish the period.
- Approximate spoken or narrative times may be spelled: `at seven`, `ten-fifteen`.
- Preserve device-interface styling when reproducing a display, such as `8:41 PM. 3 min 12 sec.`
- Spell ages in prose and dialogue; hyphenate attributive ages, as in `thirteen-year-old`.
- Use `911` in narration, records, and interface references; use `nine-one-one` when a character speaks it aloud.
- Temperatures use numerals. Retain `°F` where it appears in quoted official guidance; the surrounding prose may omit a repeatedly established unit.

## Illness, medical, institutional, and firearm terminology

- Official generic illness: `gastrointestinal illness` or `gastrointestinal outbreak`.
- Informal terms: `stomach flu`, `the purge`, and `the color`. Do not use `the purge` as an official diagnosis.
- Official supply wording: `emergency-formulary code`; `ORAL REHYDRATION SUPPORT` when reproducing the label.
- `red medicine`, `red treatment`, and `red formulation` may vary by viewpoint: family speech, general narrative, and clinical or supply context respectively.
- `recovered individuals` and `separation distress` remain lowercase in prose; capitalize only when reproducing a heading.
- Reproduce the full title as `TEMPORARY SEPARATION DISTRESS: SCHOOL SUPPORT GUIDANCE`.
- Use `Family Reunification` for the official service and `reunification desk` generically.
- Preserve established medical terms including `IV`, `pediatric observation`, `capillary refill`, `mucous membranes`, `electrolyte imbalance`, `urine output`, `contact precautions`, `foodborne`, and `symptom-free`.
- First full firearm reference: `twelve-gauge pump shotgun`; thereafter `shotgun`.
- Use `gun safe` on first descriptive use and `safe` thereafter. Use `pellets` or `shot`, never `bullets`, for the shotgun discharge.

## Dialogue, italics, headings, and found media

- Ordinary speech uses double quotation marks.
- Preserve natural character speech such as `From who?` and `To who?`; do not impose formal `whom` where it would alter voice.
- Article, upload, project, document, and internal-list titles use italics.
- Preserve intentional lowercase and italics for Brooke's project, *the color*.
- In prose, use title case for named sequence references such as `Upload Four` and `Upload Six`.
- Short texts, emails, comments, remembered source lines, and interface text may be italicized.
- Multi-line documents, feeds, notices, and transcripts use blockquotes. Do not add redundant outer quotation marks to a full blockquoted transcript.
- Bold within blockquotes is reserved for handles, labels, or interface headings.
- All-caps text is reserved for exact labels, folders, banners, and notices.
- Prologue heading: `# Prologue: Title`.
- Chapter headings: one H1 in the form `# Chapter N: Title`.
