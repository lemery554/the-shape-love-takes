# Beta Questionnaire — *The Shape Love Takes*

Please read the whole book first. Answer from impression and memory. There are no wrong answers, and "I don't know" or "I didn't notice" is useful data. Feel free to be blunt.

## A. First and last

1. In one or two sentences, what is this book about? (What you'd tell a friend.)
2. Did you finish it? If you set it down, where, and what pulled you away?
3. What is the single strongest thing about it? The single weakest?
4. Would you recommend it, and to whom?

## B. The central turn (the point of the book)

5. At what point did you start to suspect the "recovered" people were not simply recovered? Chapter or scene if you can.
6. Did you ever consciously realize that **coming back "recovered" is the bad outcome** — that survival is the horror? When, and did it land as a chill or as a shrug?
7. Did the illness feel genuinely **dangerous and deadly**, or mostly like it made people clingy? Where did the danger feel most real?
8. The cause of the illness is never explained. Did that leave you **unsettled** or **cheated**? Be honest.

## C. Brooke

9. Did you like / trust / root for Brooke? Did that change over the book?
10. Brooke does cruel things (a remark about a "real mother") and films people without consent. Did she stay sympathetic? Was there a point you lost patience with her?
11. Her habit of recording everything — did it feel like a real person's psychology, or a gimmick?

## D. The family and the fear

12. Claire's closeness (following, touching, breaking a bathroom lock) — did it read as **frightening**, or just as a clingy, traumatized kid? When did it tip, if it did?
13. Sarah, the mother — did her change land? Was there a moment you felt the mother was "gone"? Or did she just seem stressed and controlling?
14. Cooper, the dog — did he work for you? Did you fear for him?
15. Did the family's early **welcoming** of the closeness feel believable — that grief and relief would make them excuse it?

## E. Grief and the world

16. People die in this (a crossing guard from Brooke's film, a classmate, others). Did those losses land, or feel like decoration? Did the death of Mr. Alvarez do anything for you?
17. The schools, hospitals, news, and police increasingly push "stay together / don't isolate." Did that feel like plausible institutional behavior, or like a conspiracy?

## F. Anna and the ending

18. Anna (the woman at the diner, the car). When she first appeared, what did you think she was? Did that change?
19. Brooke's choice to get in Anna's car — did it feel **understandable** or **foolish**? Did you believe a smart, suspicious girl would do it?
20. The final chapter (Anna and the journal). Did the ending satisfy you, unsettle you, or disappoint you? Did the restraint work, or did you want more resolution?
21. One character's fate and the "what happens next" are left open. Did that feel deliberate and haunting, or unfinished and annoying?
22. Did the ending make you want to read a **sequel**? Would you?

## G. Pace and craft

23. Where did the book **drag** or make you want to skim? (Chapter numbers if you can — this is one of the most useful answers.)
24. Where did it **grip** you hardest?
25. The book uses embedded texts, comments, recordings, and search results. Did those help the story or pull you out of it? Too many, too few, about right?
26. Anything you had to **reread** to understand? Anything you thought was a mistake or contradiction?

## H. Emotional and sensitivity

27. What did you **feel** at the end — and a day later, what stuck with you?
28. Was anything **gratuitous** — a death, a cruelty, the gun — that felt used for shock rather than story?
29. (Especially if adoption is part of your life:) Did the adoption material and the "real mother" cruelty feel authentic and handled with care, or exploitative or wrong? Anything that read as harmful?

## I. Open floor

30. Anything else — a line you loved, a thing that annoyed you, a question you're still asking, a moment you didn't buy. Say it here.

---

_Optional: as you read, jot the chapter number anywhere you felt bored, confused, scared, or moved. Those margin marks are worth as much as the answers above._
