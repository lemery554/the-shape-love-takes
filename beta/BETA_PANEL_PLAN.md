# Beta Reader Panel Plan

Status: Active. Supports issue #26 (multi-profile beta panel) and backlog TWI-305 / TWI-306.
Manuscript at beta: ~67,180 words, Prologue + 29 chapters.

## Goal

Get honest, structured reactions from a small, deliberately varied panel before any copyedit or query package. We are testing whether the book *works* on readers, not polishing prose. The central questions the panel must answer:

1. Does the reader come to feel that **"recovery" is the wrong outcome** — and when?
2. Does the illness feel **genuinely dangerous and lethal**, not just "clingy"?
3. Does the **ambiguity** (Brooke can never prove it) intrigue rather than frustrate?
4. Does the **restrained ending** (Anna, the journal, no explanation) land — or read as a cheat?
5. Is **Brooke** sympathetic despite her cruelty and her camera compulsion?
6. Does the **grief layer** (Mr. Alvarez, the empty desks) make the closeness feel human before it feels wrong?

## Panel size and composition

Aim for **5 to 7 readers**, one per lens (a reader can hold two lenses if needed). Deliberately mixed so no single taste dominates.

| # | Reader lens | What they are for | Recruit from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Horror reader** | Does it deliver as horror? Is the dread earned? Does restraint pay off or deflate? | Horror book communities, r/horrorlit, genre newsletters |
| 2 | **Psychological / literary reader** | Character, prose, theme, the value of ambiguity and unreliability | Literary fiction circles, writing groups |
| 3 | **Thriller / pace reader** | Momentum and tension; where it drags; plot logic under pressure | Thriller readers, ARC reviewers |
| 4 | **Adoption-experience / sensitivity reader** | The adoption wound, the cruel "real mother" line, Anna's exploitation of it; authenticity and potential harm | Sensitivity-reader networks; adoptee readers |
| 5 | **YA-crossover reader** | Protagonist is 17; does the teen voice ring true; does it read adult or young-adult | YA readers/reviewers, teachers/librarians |
| 6 | **Skeptical / plot-breaker reader** | Holes, fairness of the mystery, whether the ending is satisfying and earned | Beta readers who enjoy tearing plots apart |
| 7 | **Cold general reader** | No genre allegiance: did they finish, did they care, would they recommend | Friends-of-friends outside the writing world |

## Content and sensitivity notes (share up front)

The book contains: a child's terminal illness and death (offstage), other community deaths, coercive family control, a cruel remark weaponizing adoption/abandonment, medical trauma, and a firearm discharge inside a home during a siege. No sexual content. Mark's fate and the illness's cause are intentionally unresolved. Give readers this list before they start so no one is ambushed, and flag reader 4 (adoption) especially.

## Logistics

- **Copy:** `beta/READING_COPY.md` (single file). Offer .docx/.epub/PDF on request; keep formatting plain.
- **Confidentiality:** unpublished draft; ask readers not to share, quote, or post. A light informal NDA line in the brief is enough for this stage.
- **Timeline:** 3 to 4 weeks to read ~67k words and complete the questionnaire.
- **Compensation:** name in acknowledgments; a finished copy; optionally a small gift card. Set expectations in the brief.
- **No coaching:** do not tell readers what to notice beyond the brief. Let confusion surface naturally; confusion data is the most valuable output.
- **Do not defend the book to readers.** Collect, then triage (TWI-306). Only one or two rounds; avoid endless revision.

## Triage method (after reads return, TWI-306)

1. Collect all questionnaires plus any inline margin notes.
2. Tag every comment: **confusion**, **boredom/skim**, **disbelief**, **emotional hit**, **taste**, **line edit**.
3. Weight by **pattern, not volume**: a note only one reader makes is a data point; a note three of seven make is a mandate. A single sensitivity-reader flag on harm is a mandate on its own.
4. Separate **fixable craft** (pacing, a muddy beat) from **taste** (a reader who wanted a monster movie). Do not revise toward a reader who wanted a different book.
5. Protect the load-bearing design decisions unless the panel shows they are *failing*: the unexplained mechanism, the restrained ending, Mark's open fate, the ambiguity. If multiple readers hate the ending, that is a real signal; if one does, hold.
6. Produce a ranked revision list; decide what to change before copyedit.

## Optional: simulated dry-run panel

Before recruiting humans, an AI dry-run can pre-surface likely feedback: perform an in-character read from each lens above and answer the questionnaire as that reader would, to catch obvious problems cheaply. This is a rehearsal, not a substitute for real readers — simulated reactions cannot tell you whether real people will actually care or feel harmed. Use it to fix the cheap, obvious issues first so real readers spend their attention on the subtle ones.
